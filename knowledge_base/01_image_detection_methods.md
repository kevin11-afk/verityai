# How AI-generated image detectors work

## Classifier-based detection (CNN features)

Wang, Wang, Zhang, Owens, and Efros showed in "CNN-Generated Images Are
Surprisingly Easy to Spot... for Now" (CVPR 2020) that a standard image
classifier (a ResNet-50) trained on the outputs of just one GAN generator
could, with careful data augmentation (JPEG compression and blur applied
during training), generalize surprisingly well to images from *other*
unseen GAN architectures it had never seen during training. The key
finding was that the augmentation pipeline mattered more than the
particular generator used to produce training examples: generated images
across many GAN families share low-level statistical artifacts that a
CNN can learn to pick up on even without seeing that specific generator.
The paper's title is deliberately cautionary — "for now" — because the
authors anticipated that future generators (diffusion models in
particular) would close this gap, which later work confirmed (see
"Generalization to unseen generators" below).

Source: Wang, S.-Y., Wang, O., Zhang, R., Owens, A., & Efros, A. A. (2020).
CNN-Generated Images Are Surprisingly Easy to Spot... for Now. *CVPR 2020*.
https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Wang_CNN-Generated_Images_Are_Surprisingly_Easy_to_Spot..._for_Now_CVPR_2020_paper.html

## Frequency-domain artifacts

Frank, Eisenhofer, Schönherr, Fischer, Kolossa, and Holz, in "Leveraging
Frequency Analysis for Deep Fake Image Recognition" (ICML 2020), showed
that GAN-generated images contain characteristic, visible artifacts in
the frequency domain (after a 2D Fourier transform) that are caused by
the upsampling ("transposed convolution") operations most GAN decoders
use. These grid-like frequency artifacts are usually invisible to the
naked eye in pixel space but are easy for a simple classifier to detect
once the image is transformed into the frequency domain. This is a good
example of how detectors don't need to "see" an image the way a human
does — they can exploit generator-specific implementation artifacts that
have nothing to do with the image's content.

Source: Frank, J., Eisenhofer, T., Schönherr, L., Fischer, A., Kolossa, D.,
& Holz, T. (2020). Leveraging Frequency Analysis for Deep Fake Image
Recognition. *ICML 2020*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08685

## Diffusion models change the picture

Diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, DALL-E, Midjourney) do not use the
same transposed-convolution upsampling as GANs, so GAN-era frequency
artifacts are a much weaker signal against them. Corvi, Cozzolino,
Zingarini, Poggi, Nagano, and Verdoliva, in "On the Detection of Synthetic
Images Generated by Diffusion Models" (ICASSP 2023), evaluated several
detectors originally designed for GAN images against diffusion-model
outputs and found a measurable drop in detection accuracy, confirming that
detectors tuned on one generator family do not automatically transfer to
a structurally different one. This is the core reason VerityAI's own
report always names which generator family the selected detector was
trained to recognize, and flags newer or structurally different
generators as a known gap rather than implying universal coverage.

Source: Corvi, R., Cozzolino, D., Zingarini, G., Poggi, G., Nagano, K., &
Verdoliva, L. (2023). On the Detection of Synthetic Images Generated by
Diffusion Models. *ICASSP 2023*. https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00680
